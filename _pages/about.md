I am a PhD candidate in the [Division of Biostatistics](https://www.mcw.edu/departments/biostatistics) at the [Medical College of Wisconsin](https://www.mcw.edu/), advised by Dr. Anjishnu Banerjee. I obtained my MS in Mathematics (Statistics emphasis) from [Boise State University](https://www.boisestate.edu/) and my BS in Statistics from the [University of Dhaka](https://www.du.ac.bd/).

My dissertation is on Bayesian variable selection for high-dimensional data. The main project is BRISK, a rank-based method built on a robust permutation kernel, with knockoffs to control the false discovery rate. I have also worked on borrowing external information through mixture priors.

As a graduate research assistant, I work with MCW investigators on clinical and genomic studies. Much of it is survival analysis, competing risks included, for transplant and oncology projects. I have also analyzed transcriptomic, proteomic, and microbiome data where the number of features far exceeds the number of patients. Right now I am working with Dr. Duo Yu on GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy after kidney transplant.

Outside of research, I enjoy watching movies, trivia nights, traveling, reading, and spending time with my two kitties. I love exploring new places, learning about different cultures, and finding creative inspiration both in science and in everyday life.
